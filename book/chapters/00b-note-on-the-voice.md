# A Note on the Voice

This book is narrated in the first person by someone who does not exist.

The Preface describes the machinery that produced these pages and the rule it enforces: every fact, dose, study, and attribution traces to a specific person on a specific podcast on a specific date. Nothing factual was invented. The narrator was. The narrator is a device, chosen deliberately: a non-specialist who worked through the material and reports back to you as an equal rather than lecturing you as a patient. Facts are the book's obligation. The voice carrying them is a choice about how to be read.

The scale is specific. Nearly seven hundred episodes are cited in the endnotes, roughly a thousand hours of recorded conversation, drawn from the much larger corpus of nine thousand episodes the Preface describes. Reaching those seven hundred meant screening many times that number, because most of what you listen to repeats what you already heard, contradicts it without acknowledging the contradiction, or turns out to be an advertisement wearing a lab coat. Over three thousand hours is a conservative estimate of the total labor. That is a year and a half of full-time work, or six years of evenings. The narrator is a device precisely because no one was ever going to do that.

The narrator has no life, no history, and no body, and has tried none of this. Where the text sounds like experience, it is a way of speaking. Nothing in this book is a personal testimonial.

What the narrator does have is a temperature. The prose warms up around findings that are large, replicated, and echoed by independent speakers. It goes flat and skeptical around claims resting on one practitioner, one small study, or a sponsorship. That shift is deliberate. Read the heat of a passage as a reading of the evidence behind it.
